# Story and emotional structure

Extract genre, theme, character motivation, obstacle, and intended feeling. Write one causal sentence: “Because [motivation], the character tries [action], faces [obstacle], and changes through [choice].” Expand creatively without replacing the user's premise.

Map beginning → development → climax → resolution to visible beats. A short film may combine stages in one shot. Make the final image reflect a change from the opening: distance to connection, fear to resolve, stillness to departure. Avoid explaining invisible backstory with long dialogue.

Genre choices:
- Emotional/romantic drama: hesitation, specific gesture, response, emotional hold.
- Action: readable objective, obstacle, directional movement, consequence.
- Fantasy/dark fantasy: one clear world rule, its cost, a meaningful choice.
- Slice of life: a small concrete action and an observed emotional shift.
- Music video: image motifs and movement tied to supplied musical cues; do not invent an exact beat grid without tempo/audio evidence.
- Trailer: setup, escalation, compelling unresolved image; avoid compressing a whole film into a few seconds.
- General production: use the requested product/story goal and style instead of imposing anime drama.

When time is scarce, remove secondary characters, locations, and repeated actions. Keep the emotional turn. Scene count serves the story unless the user fixes it explicitly.
