# Thai dialogue and sound

Use Thai for actual spoken lines by default, English for audio/visual direction. Preserve user-supplied wording when feasible. Assign each line a stable speaker ID, exact quoted text, absolute time window, vocal character (gender only if given or appropriate to the fictional design), emotion, pace, and delivery. Do not assign a voice identity solely from appearance.

Estimate speech by reading aloud or using available timing tools. As a rough planning heuristic, conversational Thai may use roughly 3–5 syllables per second; emotion, consonants, pauses, and delivery vary greatly. Thai whitespace is not a reliable word/syllable counter. Reserve breathing and reaction time; do not label an estimate as measured. Prefer short lines, e.g. “กลับบ้านกันนะ” with a generous pause rather than a rushed monologue.

If a line does not fit, shorten it while preserving intent, move it to a longer scene, or simplify surrounding action. Do not silently alter exact mandatory dialogue. Use one speaker at a time unless an intentional overlap is clearly timed. Name the visible mouth to animate and keep other mouths at rest. Avoid speech under an obscured face when visible lip synchronization is essential.

Layer sound: continuous ambience; event-aligned effects (contact, footsteps, fabric); music supporting the arc; dialogue above music. Use specific entrances and fades, and let meaningful silence breathe. Do not add spoken narration or subtitles unless requested. A quoted Thai line is speech, not on-screen text.

Native Thai voices, sound generation, and lip sync depend on the chosen provider/model and must be verified. Otherwise keep the script and timing as a postproduction audio brief, render visuals separately, and align recorded/generated speech and sound in editing. Do not promise perfect phoneme alignment.
